Why

A failure here isn't a bad quarter

In oil and gas, chemicals, power and the rest of heavy industry, it can mean a fire, an explosion, a release or people not going home.

What

Technology that is new to the people adopting it

Robots, drones, sensors, artificial intelligence and the control systems that run the plant. Much of it doesn't upgrade something they already do. It's something they have never done before.

Trap

Adopting it because it is new

Because it demos well, because a competitor has it, because it photographs well on a site visit. That kind of adoption rarely lasts - and here it can be dangerous.

A new technology earns its place when three things are true

  1. it solves a problem the operation actually has;
  2. the value of solving it has been worked out honestly;
  3. it has been shown to work reliably, on a real asset, in real conditions - not just in a demonstration.

Two out of three feels like success... until it doesn't

Most disasters don't happen to bad operators. They happen to good ones that were missing one thing.

Insight

Clarity, but no change.

The risk has been identified and the report is well written - then it goes back into the drawer. The inquiry doesn't find ignorance. It finds a warning on file.

Missing: Evolutionary Transformation

Momentum

Action, but in silos.

Every team is in control of its own patch and milestones are being hit. The signals are never joined up, so "it passed the test" gets heard as "it will work in service".

Missing: Integrated Signal Intelligence

Precision

Control, but blind spots.

Well run, systematic, competent - against the hazards somebody thought to look for. The thing that mattered never reached anyone's attention.

Missing: Total Awareness

Three standards, not one

It's easy to think the job is done when a system is fit for purpose.

Where most technology stops

Fit for purpose

It does what the specification says - and a certificate proves it.

The harder standard

Fit for service

It works on a live asset, in real conditions, for as long as the asset needs it - and the operator's risk case stands behind it.

Apex

Sustainably fit for service

Not fit on the day of handover - fit on the thousandth day, when nobody is watching any more.
