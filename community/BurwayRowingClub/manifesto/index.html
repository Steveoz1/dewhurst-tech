We are a river club

We are not a school club. We are not a university club. We are not one of the big competitive clubs with a professional coaching staff and a selection queue. We are a town club — volunteer-coached, volunteer-led, and paid for by the people who row here.

Everything good about this club comes from that, and so does everything hard about it. Both are worth saying out loud.

What we are — and what we are not

Being honest about the second column is a kindness, not a limitation. Every line in it is a deliberate choice, and each one is what makes something in the first column possible.

What we are

A club anyone can join and nobody has to earn their way into

Our members are twelve years old and eighty years old. Some have raced at the Olympics. Some got into a boat for the first time last month. The distance between those two people is smaller here than almost anywhere in the sport.

Friendly and competitive — in that order, and we mean both

Friendly first is not a lowering of ambition. It is the condition that makes ambition survivable in a club run entirely by volunteers. We race hard, we want to win, and we go to the pub afterwards with the people we raced against.

A tribe before we are a squad

The people who make this club are the ones who turn up — in February as well as June, to rig boats, coach juniors, tow trailers and stand on a cold bank with a stopwatch. Turning up is the currency here.

A club that develops people, not just crews

The junior who joins at twelve and is still here at twenty-five, coaching. The adult who could barely swim and is now racing at Marlow. Those are our results, as much as any pennant.

Ambitious, and unapologetic about it

Tom Barras rowed here. We came back to the Head of the River Race after twenty-four years, had our boat damaged on race morning, borrowed one, and still beat around fifty crews. We are small. We are not modest about what small clubs can do.

Built to still be here in 2121

Which means the way we run has to survive any one of us moving away, getting ill, or simply getting tired.

What we are not

Not a professional programme

We cannot offer a bespoke, individually coached, twelve-sessions-a-week programme. If you want to chase that level from Burway you are entirely welcome, and we will give you water, a good boat, honest technical feedback and our full support. What we cannot give you is a full-time coach — because that coach is the only one the rest of the club has.

Not a club you can only belong to if you are fast

Ability does not determine your standing here. There is a boat and a crew for you whether you are targeting a championship or targeting getting round without stopping.

Not a club you can belong to without turning up

This is the honest flip side. Seats in crews go to the people training with those crews. If life takes you away, you remain one of ours and the door stays open — but we will not ask eight people who trained all winter to move aside on the morning. That is not a rule against anyone. It is the same rule for everyone.

Not a club that runs on one person

When something only works because a particular person makes it work, we treat that as a risk to fix, not an achievement to celebrate. That person deserves better than being irreplaceable.

Not a club where shouting decides anything

See below. It is the part we most need to mean.

What you can expect from us

A plan you can see

Published training and a published race calendar, far enough ahead that you can organise your life around them.

A straight answer

About selection, about what your squad can expect, and about what we cannot do. We would rather disappoint you in September than in June.

A coach who knows your name

And knows what you are working on this month.

A safe river

Safety and safeguarding are not negotiable, not a paperwork exercise, and never quietly set aside because a crew is keen.

An invitation

To the socials, the camping weekend, the bank on race day, and the committee. Especially if you only joined last week.

Somewhere to raise a worry

Without having to confront anybody in the moment.

And what we ask of you

Turn up

More than anything else, this is it.

Take your turn

Carry someone else's boat. Do a bank shift. Drive. Wash the trailer. Everyone does, including the fastest people here.

Say when you can't

Early. Nobody minds a member who is honest about availability; the club only struggles with the ones who go quiet.

Look after the new person

You remember what that first session felt like.

Leave it better

Blades away, boat clean, gate shut, lights off.

How we carry ourselves

We are a club of volunteers who care a great deal. Race days are long, they start before dawn, the trailer gets stuck in traffic, the entry changes, the umpire's call looks wrong from the bank. Caring is exactly what makes those moments hard to swallow, and none of us should pretend we have never felt it rise.

But when you hold a role at this club — coach, captain, committee, president — you do not get to be off duty in public. In club kit at an event, you are not there as yourself. You are what Burway looks like to a fourteen-year-old in our own squad, to a parent deciding whether to sign up again next year, and to a volunteer from another club who will remember us for a long time on the strength of ninety seconds.

Disagreement is welcome here — a club with no argument in it has no ideas in it. What has no place is the volume and the audience. A senior person losing their temper in front of others does not read as passion. It reads as permission, and it lands hardest on the people with the least standing to object.

  1. We disagree with ideas, never with people.
  2. Nothing gets said loudly in public that should be said quietly in private.
  3. The juniors are watching. They copy what we do, not what we say.
  4. Officials, volunteers and other clubs get our best manners — especially when the decision goes against us.
  5. When we get it wrong, we say so — quickly and plainly. It is worth more than any policy.
  6. We check on whoever took the hit.
  7. Anyone can call it. Any member, of any age or standing, can say “not here, not now” to anyone else — including the most senior people in this club — and will be backed for it.
  8. We speak well of Burway in public and settle our differences in private.

None of us is above this list. That is the point of writing it down together rather than aiming it at anyone.

A hundred and five years of people carrying boats down to the water.
Let's make sure someone is still doing it in a hundred more.
