Value & ROI Calculator

Cost the whole system, not just the kit.

Build the value case for scaling inspection, maintenance and surveillance robotics - and the OT infrastructure beneath them - from a pilot into live operations. Seven short steps compare the way the work is done today with one or more proposed solutions, across technology, process, people and performance. The return is shown in money and in hours freed.

    More detail: why the day rate matters so much

    Most of the value in these cases is people's time - hours on site, planning, permits, reporting. Every hour in the model is valued at the hourly rate above, so a change to the day cost moves every result.

    If your organisation uses different rates for different crews, use an average here and enter any external contractor costs as money, not hours, in steps 4 and 5.

    Step 3 of 7

    Describe the use case

    One calculation covers one use case: a single piece of work you want to do differently. A clear name makes the report easy to share with the people who will approve it.

    Step 4 of 7

    Cost the way the work is done today

    This is your baseline. It is usually mostly people's time and contractor costs, with little or no new equipment. Open each area, fill in what applies and leave the rest at zero.

    How to fill in a line

    Cost is money spent: equipment, contractors, hire, licences. Hours are your own people's time; the tool values them at the hourly rate from step 2.

    One-off means you pay it once to get started. Each year means it comes round again every year. Most baseline costs are "each year".

    Anything that doesn't fit a line? Use "Add a line" in that area. Lost or deferred production is a common one: the examples leave it out on purpose, because only you know what a day of your asset is worth.

    Step 5 of 7

    Cost the proposed solution

    Now enter what it takes to stand the new way of working up and run it. This is where cases are usually too thin: the robot is costed, but the network connection, the cyber-security work, the change process and the training are not. Each area prompts you for them.

    Step 6 of 7

    Your results

    Each solution is compared with today's way of working. Check the figures that drive the answer before you share it - the two that are usually challenged first are the hours saved and the one-off cost.

    Cumulative cost over time

    Show the figures as a table

    How the results are worked out

    Annual cost = everything marked "each year", with hours valued at your hourly rate. Annual saving = today's annual cost minus the solution's annual cost.

    One-off investment = everything marked "one-off" in the solution, less anything one-off in today's baseline. Payback = one-off investment ÷ annual saving. Return over the period = (annual saving × years) ÷ one-off investment, shown as value for each unit invested.

    The six headline figures are not discounted. The chart's Discounted view, the net present value and the discounted payback use the discount rate from step 2, applied month by month.
